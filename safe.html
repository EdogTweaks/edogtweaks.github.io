Why you see the warning

Windows doesn't know us yet

The box says "Windows protected your PC", and it comes from a Windows feature called SmartScreen. It warns about apps that haven't been downloaded by many people yet, or that don't have a paid code-signing certificate behind them.

Edog Tweaks is a small, new developer and our apps aren't code-signed yet, so you'll see it the first time you open one. It doesn't mean a virus was found. SmartScreen is saying "I don't recognize this", not "this is bad".

Here's the honest part: real malware can trigger that same box, so being careful about it is smart. That's why the rest of this page gives you ways to check instead of asking you to trust us.

Don't take our word for it

Check it yourself in a minute

You don't need to know anything technical. Any one of these is worth doing, and all three together take about a minute.

CHECK 01

Scan it with Defender

Before you unzip it, right-click the zip in your Downloads folder. On Windows 11 pick Show more options, then Scan with Microsoft Defender.

CHECK 02

Compare the fingerprint

Every file has a unique code called a SHA-256 hash. Change one byte and the whole code changes. Right-click an empty spot in Downloads, pick Open in Terminal, and run this with your file's name:

Get-FileHash .\Edog-Macro-v1.4.1-portable.zip

The Hash it prints must match the one in Scan results exactly. If it doesn't, delete the file and tell us.

CHECK 03

Get a second opinion

Upload the zip to VirusTotal, a free Google-owned site that runs a file through dozens of antivirus engines. Anyone can use it, and we don't control the results.

New, unsigned apps can get an odd flag or two from smaller engines, so look at how many flag it and what they call it. If it worries you, ask us before running it.

Where to get the real files: your Payhip purchase email, the update prompt inside the app, or the #downloads channel in our Discord. We'll never DM you a download. If someone does, it isn't us.

Straight answers

The things you might still be wondering

Does a clean scan prove an app is 100% safe?

No, and no scanner can promise that for any app. A scan is one piece of evidence. That's why this page also lists what the apps do and how to check the files for yourself.

Why aren't the apps signed?

Signing means paying for a certificate tied to a verified identity. It proves who published a file, though it doesn't prove the file is safe. We're a small developer and haven't done that yet, which is why Windows says "Unknown publisher".

My antivirus flagged it. What now?

Macros press keys for you and Edog Tweaks changes Windows settings. Malware does similar things, so some antivirus programs can flag tools like these based on behavior alone.

Don't turn your antivirus off. Compare the fingerprint first, then tell us in the Discord's support channel which antivirus it was and the name it showed, and we'll look into it.

Why is the app so big?

Each app is about 125 to 155 MB because it carries its own copy of Microsoft .NET, the software it's built on. That way you don't have to install anything else to run it.

Does safe for my PC mean allowed in my game?

No, those are separate questions. This page is about your PC. Many games and most tournaments don't allow macros, so check the rules where you play. You use them at your own risk.

How do I remove an app?

In Edog Tweaks, press Undo everything first. In the others, turn off start with Windows if you turned it on, and in Edog Crosshair switch off the custom cursor if you used it. Then close the app and delete it, plus its settings folder: press Win + R, type %APPDATA%, and delete the Edog folder for that app. If you installed the ViGEmBus driver and want it gone too, remove it in Windows' Installed apps.
